# Guide — Business analysis method

**Purpose:** Understand how a listed business creates durable economic value **before** estimating its share price.

## Sequence

1. **Define the security.** Identify exact exchange ticker, share class, parent company, subsidiaries, shares outstanding and minority interests.
2. **Map revenue engines.** List products, customer types, distribution channels, prices/yields/fees and relevant unit economics.
3. **Follow the cash.** Which activities create profit, which require capital, and what cash can legally flow to the listed shareholder?
4. **Understand competition.** Identify rivals per business segment, then gather comparable metrics for matching periods.
5. **Test advantage, don't assume it.** Describe a measurable reason for outperformance and evidence that could disprove it.
6. **Identify fragilities.** Credit, regulatory, leverage, related-party, macroeconomic, technology and concentration risks.
7. **Separate analysis from valuation.** A high-quality company can still be unattractive at a particular market price.

## Key questions for SCAP

- Where does the insurer's value come from, and how much belongs to SCAP?
- Is finance-company asset quality and funding stable?
- Do brokerage and asset management contribute consistently positive net income?
- What amount of cash reaches the holding company after obligations?
- What historical and current evidence would challenge the investment thesis?

## Evidence discipline

For every important claim, capture **source, reporting period, publication date, page/note, scope (parent vs group vs subsidiary), units and confidence status**. Mark unknown information **OPEN**, not zero.
